# Crit 7 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was using the planner myself, as a student, once the tests
were passing. At that point the suite had over 400 tests, and none of them
could tell me that COMP3770 runs over two semesters, or that a list of
postgraduate clashes gets in the way for an undergraduate. The catalogue
listed COMP3770 as 6 units, and the agent trusted it. However, I knew it was
a year-long course, and its own summary said so too. That one disagreement
became the rule for every annual course. Most of the features I added in the
last stretch came the same way, from planning my actual degree and noticing
where the tool got in the way.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to be a developer who plans for the build going wrong as well. This
week I wrote a recovery protocol when I could see the course credit running
low, and about fifteen minutes later every background agent stopped at once.
Each track had committed in small steps, and the protocol said what to check
first. This is why the next session could start by sorting out what had
survived, and did not have to guess.

Credit also turned out to be an engineering constraint like any other. It
decided which model did which job, how much scope was realistic, and when to
cut the catalogue from every ANU course down to two colleges. I used to see
running out as a session going wrong. Now I think of it as something to plan
around, the same way I would plan around a slow network or a full disk.
